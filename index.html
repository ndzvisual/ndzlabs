02 — The Core Idea

AI doesn't have to make humans unnecessary to make them more productive.

We believe the most valuable AI systems are not necessarily the ones that replace the most human work, but the ones that remove the mechanical parts of complex workflows and give people more room for creativity, judgment, strategy and decision-making.

The objective is not to make humans work less, it's to help them work better.

By delegating execution to agentic systems, humans can spend more of their limited attention on the parts of the process where their involvement matters most.

AI handles more of the execution. Humans retain more of the agency.

03 — The Question

AI can already execute many parts of complex workflows.

The harder question is what we should ask it to do

  • How much autonomy should an agent have?
  • When is a specialized team better than a single agent?
  • When does automation actually improve human work?
  • And what happens when execution becomes abundant?

NDZ Labs explores those questions by building real systems and testing them in real workflows.

The goal isn't to replace the human. It's to increase what the human can think, create and accomplish.

05 — The Agentic Architecture

There is no single architecture for agentic systems.

The right structure depends on the work being delegated, the level of autonomy required and where human judgment needs to remain in the loop.

NDZ Labs experiments with different architectural patterns to understand what works, where they fail and when one approach is preferable to another.

Declarative systems

Explicit responsibilities, contracts and workflows define how agents interact and what each stage is expected to produce.

Skills-based agents

A capable agent can acquire specialized behaviors through skills without requiring an independent agent for every responsibility.

Agentic teams

Multiple specialized agents can reproduce organizational roles such as Product Management, Design, Development and QA.

Human-led systems

The human remains an explicit component of the architecture, defining direction, making critical decisions and intervening where autonomy stops being useful.

The architecture is part of the experiment

06 — The Agnostic Layer

The intelligence can change. The system remains.

NDZ Labs systems are designed to remain independent from the environment in which they operate.
Models, providers, development environments, tools and infrastructure can change without redefining the system itself.
This separation allows us to move experiments across different environments without rebuilding the architecture around a specific vendor or workflow.
More importantly, it keeps the research focused on the system and the problem it is designed to solve, rather than on the particular technology used to run it.

LLM

LLM agnostic

The development workflow is not built around a specific model provider.

Different models can execute different parts of the system without redefining the product process.

IDE

IDE agnostic

The system does not depend on a particular development environment.

The orchestration layer lives above the tools used to write the code.

LANG

Language agnostic

The contracts describe what the system needs to achieve, rather than prescribing a specific programming language.

The implementation stack can change without changing the underlying development methodology.
